Terms & Conditions

Terms governing authorised use of the encrypted Admin Control patient-administration platform.

Effective date: 15 August 2026
Important: Admin Control supports surgery administration and record management. It does not replace a practice's own patient notices, consent processes, POPIA programme, PAIA obligations, clinical policies, professional duties or legal advice. Each practice remains responsible for determining the lawful basis, purpose and retention rules for the information it processes.

1. Acceptance and authorised use

By accessing or using Admin Control, the medical practice and each authorised staff member agree to these Terms & Conditions. The service may be used only for legitimate practice administration and record-management activities within the surgery workspace to which the user has been authorised.

2. Purpose and clinical limitation

Admin Control is an administrative patient-record and reception workflow system for locating patient files, managing beneficiaries and medical-aid information, recording consultation administration, searching records, creating permitted exports and maintaining encrypted backups.

The platform is not a diagnostic system, clinical decision-support system, emergency service, electronic prescribing system or substitute for professional medical judgement. Clinical decisions must never be based solely on the platform.

3. Practice responsibilities

The practice generally determines why and how patient information is collected and used in its workspace and must ensure that such processing is lawful, adequate, relevant and not excessive for the intended purpose.

  • Restrict access to staff who genuinely require it for authorised duties.
  • Keep patient and beneficiary information accurate and update it when reasonably necessary.
  • Maintain appropriate patient-facing notices, permissions, lawful grounds and records of processing where required.
  • Maintain retention, correction, deletion, incident-response and information-security procedures appropriate to the practice.
  • Avoid unnecessary clinical or highly sensitive detail in reception notes.
  • Review access promptly when a staff member leaves the practice or changes role.

4. Accounts, devices and credentials

Users must protect login credentials, use strong passwords, sign out from shared devices and prevent unauthorised access to devices used for Admin Control. Credentials should not be shared between staff members. Suspected credential theft, device compromise or unauthorised account access must be reported promptly to the practice administrator.

5. Client-side encryption and surgery passphrase

Protected patient content is encrypted in the browser before it is stored in Supabase. The surgery encryption passphrase is used to unlock the surgery data-encryption key in the browser and is not included in database records or encrypted backups.

  • The practice must choose a strong, unique passphrase and keep it in a secure recovery location under its control.
  • The passphrase must not be sent through insecure channels or stored in ordinary reception notes.
  • If the passphrase is lost and no authorised recovery copy exists, encrypted patient content may become permanently inaccessible.
  • KM Digital Labs, Supabase or another infrastructure provider may be unable to recover encrypted content without the surgery's passphrase or an authorised unlocked session.
  • Encryption reduces exposure of stored content but does not protect against every risk, including a compromised authorised device, malicious browser extension, stolen credentials, social engineering or an already-unlocked session.

6. Workspace separation and access controls

Records are assigned to surgery workspaces and database Row Level Security is used to restrict authenticated access according to workspace membership. Users may not attempt to bypass, probe, disable or circumvent access controls or attempt to access another practice's data.

7. Prohibited use

Users may not use Admin Control for unlawful processing, unauthorised surveillance, credential sharing, deliberate disclosure of patient information, malware, security testing without permission, attempts to gain unauthorised access, or any activity that compromises patient privacy, encryption, system security or service availability.

8. PDF exports and encrypted backups

Downloaded files become the practice's responsibility once created on an authorised device. Patient-register PDFs are readable documents and must be protected accordingly. Encrypted JSON backups contain encrypted records and the wrapped surgery key but do not contain the surgery passphrase. The practice must securely store, transfer, retain and destroy exports and backups.

9. Third-party infrastructure

Admin Control may rely on Supabase and other hosting, network or browser infrastructure. Third-party service availability, regional hosting, authentication and technical metadata may be outside the direct control of KM Digital Labs. The practice should ensure that its chosen configuration and service agreements are suitable for its legal and operational requirements.

10. Security incidents

Users must promptly report suspected loss, unauthorised access, disclosure, malware, stolen credentials, exposed exports or other security incidents to the practice administrator. The practice remains responsible for its legal assessment and notifications as responsible party where applicable. Where KM Digital Labs acts as an operator and becomes aware of a relevant security compromise involving practice data, incident handling should follow the applicable service agreement and legal duties.

11. Fees, support and service changes

Features may be updated, replaced or restricted where reasonably necessary for security, compatibility, reliability or legal compliance.

Privacy Policy

How the encrypted Admin Control platform processes information used by authorised medical practices.

Effective date: 15 August 2026
Scope: This policy describes the platform's data handling and security design. Each medical practice remains responsible for its own patient-facing privacy notice and for ensuring that its processing complies with the Protection of Personal Information Act 4 of 2013 (POPIA), applicable health-profession rules and other legal requirements. This policy is not a certification of compliance.

1. Privacy roles

The medical practice ordinarily decides the purpose and means of processing its patient records and will generally act as the responsible party for that information. KM Digital Labs and infrastructure providers may act as operators or service providers when processing information on the practice's documented instructions. The applicable service agreement should define these roles and responsibilities.

2. Information processed

Depending on the features used, Admin Control may process:

  • Staff account information such as name, email address, authentication identifiers and workspace membership.
  • Practice information such as surgery name, configuration and branding.
  • Patient information such as full name, identity number, file number, medical aid, medical-aid membership number and plan/option.
  • Beneficiary information such as name, relationship and file number.
  • Consultation administration such as visit date, visit type, person consulted and reception notes.
  • Technical, security and audit-related information needed to authenticate users, maintain database relationships, enforce access controls, diagnose faults and protect the service.

3. Purpose of processing

Information is processed to provide authorised surgery administration, including patient-file retrieval, beneficiary management, consultation history, record accuracy, permitted register exports, encrypted backups, authentication, workspace separation, support and security.

4. Data minimisation and sensitive information

Patient, identity, medical-aid and health-related information may be sensitive or special personal information. Practices should collect and enter only information that is reasonably necessary for a defined legitimate purpose. Reception notes should be concise and administrative unless the practice has an approved lawful purpose and appropriate safeguards for additional clinical information.

5. Children and beneficiaries

Beneficiary records may relate to children. The practice is responsible for ensuring that such information is processed only where an appropriate lawful basis or authorisation exists and with any additional safeguards required by applicable law.

6. Client-side encryption

Protected patient content is encrypted in the user's browser before transmission to Supabase using client-side encryption. The browser unlocks the surgery data-encryption key only after an authorised user provides the surgery encryption passphrase. The passphrase itself is not stored in the patient database or included in encrypted backups.

Structural metadata required for authentication, workspace membership, database relationships, security enforcement and application operation may remain available to infrastructure services. Client-side encryption is therefore an important security safeguard but does not mean that every piece of application metadata is encrypted.

7. Encryption passphrase and recovery

The surgery controls its encryption passphrase and is responsible for securing an authorised recovery copy. If the passphrase is lost and no recovery copy or authorised unlocked session is available, protected records may become permanently unreadable. Support personnel should never ask a user to send the surgery passphrase through ordinary email, chat or another insecure channel.

8. Access controls and confidentiality

Access is intended to be limited to authenticated users associated with the relevant surgery workspace. The platform combines authentication, database Row Level Security and client-side encryption. Practices must also secure endpoints, browsers, staff credentials and physical access because an authorised unlocked device can display decrypted patient content.

9. Supabase, hosting and cross-border processing

Admin Control uses Supabase for authentication and database services. Encrypted patient payloads and operational metadata may be processed or stored by Supabase according to the selected project region and service configuration. The practice should verify its hosting region and assess any cross-border processing or transfer obligations applicable to its records.

10. PDF exports and encrypted backups

The standard patient-register PDF is generated locally from decrypted browser data and is designed to contain patient names and file numbers only. Identity numbers, medical-aid membership numbers, beneficiary details and consultation notes are excluded from the standard register export. Encrypted JSON backups contain ciphertext and the wrapped surgery key; they do not include the surgery encryption passphrase.

Once a readable PDF is downloaded, it is no longer protected by the database encryption layer. The practice must protect exported files with appropriate device, storage, transfer, access and destruction controls.

11. Retention, correction and deletion

The practice determines appropriate retention periods subject to applicable healthcare, professional, contractual and privacy requirements. Personal information should not be retained longer than authorised or necessary for the purpose for which it is processed, except where retention is required or permitted by law. Correction, deletion, restriction or destruction requests must be assessed by the responsible practice.

12. Data-subject rights and requests

Patients or other data subjects should direct requests concerning access to, correction of, deletion of, or objections relating to their practice records to the medical practice that collected the information. The practice should route requests through its Information Officer or designated privacy contact and respond according to applicable law.

13. Security safeguards

The platform is designed to use reasonable technical safeguards, including authenticated access, tenant/workspace isolation, Row Level Security and client-side encryption for protected patient content. Security also depends on strong passphrases, secure accounts, patched devices, safe browsers, appropriate permissions, staff training and sound operating procedures. No technical system can eliminate all security risk.

14. Security compromises and notification

If there are reasonable grounds to believe that personal information has been accessed or acquired by an unauthorised person, the practice should immediately activate its incident-response process. Where the practice is the responsible party, it remains responsible for assessing and making notifications required by POPIA. Where a service provider acts as an operator, it should notify the responsible party in accordance with applicable law and the service agreement.

15. Sharing, sale and advertising

Patient information is not intended to be sold or used for behavioural advertising. Information should be disclosed only where necessary to provide the service, on the practice's lawful instructions, or where disclosure is required or permitted by law.

16. Information Regulator and complaints

Data subjects may have rights to complain to the Information Regulator (South Africa) where they believe their personal information has been processed unlawfully. Each practice should provide the identity and contact details of its Information Officer or designated privacy contact in its own patient-facing privacy material.

17. Changes to this policy

This Privacy Policy may be updated when the service, encryption design, security practices, infrastructure or applicable legal requirements change. The effective date above identifies the version currently presented in the application.
